# Independent Jam Stage candidate review — Krios `dunk-power` attempt01

**Verdict: suitable for another calibration trial; not ready for registration.**

I reviewed all 60 candidate captures and the report in this directory. The captures use the current Jam Stage renderer, show all 15 selected frames at 100% and 120%, both facings, and report no selector mismatch or browser error. Krios remains recognizable with his red skin, curled ram horns, beard, muscular human anatomy, and black/ember kit. The complete sprite stays within frame throughout the sampled poses. The sequence reads as approach, jump, overhead gather, forward drive, release/follow-through, and landing.

The separate ball track is close in several frames, but its overhead contact needs adjustment. In frames 5–7, the ball drifts laterally away from the raised hands, most visibly at frame06; in frames08–09 the ball is also slightly detached from the forward-reaching hands. Refine the height/forward coordinates and compare the transition from the high gather into descent at native scale. The source reviewers' concern about close neighboring poses remains most relevant at frames05–07 and frames09–11; these differences are readable as a sequence, but the release should be checked in motion before accepting the atlas.

The capture report explicitly says the athlete elevation is manually staged and this is a visual calibration only. It is not a live dunk, rim-contact, scoring, or landing result. The current samples put Krios at midcourt, so they do not establish correct basket-relative release placement. Next review should run a real Krios dunk through normal simulation with the candidate art and track loaded before game start, verify the tick21 release/score/landing, then capture registered-runtime art only after those checks pass.

I recommend carrying attempt01 into one refined candidate-track trial, preserving this attempt as the baseline. Do not register the source or the track based on these captures alone.
